## 1. Purpose
Govern future recommendations for students seeking institutions, campuses, programs and educational opportunities.

## 2. Search vs Recommendation
Search primarily matches explicit queries. Recommendations may use declared preferences and contextual signals. Recommendation output is not automatically a quality ranking.

## 3. Inputs
Potential inputs include location, study level, discipline, delivery mode, admission timing, voluntarily supplied budget and other explicit preferences.

## 4. Evidence
Recommendations must be grounded in governed canonical data and clearly identified derived signals.

## 5. Transparency
Users should be able to understand major reasons an item was recommended, such as program, location or stated-preference matches.

## 6. Community Signals
Student/teacher recommendations and ratings may be one signal. They remain community-derived and must not become institutional fact.

## 7. Ranking Interaction
A governed ranking may be an input, but recommendation output remains distinct from ranking output.

## 8. Missing Data
Unknown fees, scholarships, ratings or other optional fields must not automatically make an item unsuitable unless the user explicitly requires that information.

## 9. Personalization
Use only permitted, appropriately obtained user information. Users should retain control over preference inputs.

## 10. Sensitive Inference
Do not infer sensitive personal characteristics merely to generate educational recommendations.

## 11. AI
AI may interpret natural-language preferences and explain results, but must remain grounded in governed data and disclose material uncertainty.

## 12. Commercial Separation
Paid placement or sponsorship must not silently masquerade as neutral recommendation logic.

## 13. Evaluation
Future evaluation may consider relevance, factual correctness, diversity, coverage and inappropriate-recommendation rates.

## 14. Non-Approval of Implementation
No algorithm, scoring formula, model, AI provider or personalization engine is selected here.
